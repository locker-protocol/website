Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 23, 2026

In one sentence

Locker Protocol Wallet is a non-custodial browser extension. Your private keys are generated and stored on your air-gapped device and never touch this extension, our servers, or your browser. We do not run analytics, we do not track you, and we do not collect, sell, or share any personal data. The extension does reach a small number of third-party services to do its job, and one of ours to fetch the optional offline AI assistant; section 4 lists every one of them, and section 5 says what they receive and how your consent is asked and withdrawn.

1. Who this covers

This policy applies to the Locker Protocol Wallet browser extension for Chrome, Brave, Microsoft Edge and Firefox, and to this website (lockerprotocol.com). The extension is free and open-source software published by the Locker Protocol project.

2. We never handle your keys

Locker is an air-gapped, watch-only wallet. Signing happens on a separate device (the Locker Vault), and signatures are carried back to the extension as QR codes. The extension holds no private keys, no seed phrases, and no passwords able to derive them. There is nothing secret for us, or for an attacker who compromises this extension, to steal.

3. What the extension stores, and where

Everything below is kept locally in your browser only (the extension's own storage.local area). None of it is transmitted to us. You can erase all of it at any time by removing the extension.

  • •Public account addresses you import from your vault (public data by nature).
  • •Contacts / address book labels you choose to save.
  • •Preferences: selected network, display currency, language, and which sites you have connected to.
  • •A local lock password you set to gate the extension UI. It protects access to the interface; it does not encrypt or unlock any key, because there are none.

4. Network requests

To show balances and broadcast the transactions you have already signed, the extension talks to public infrastructure. This is the complete list, and what each one can see:

  • •RPC nodes for the networks you use, to read balances and submit signed transactions. Public endpoints by default; you can point a network at your own.
  • •The dApps you visit, which receive your public address once you connect, exactly as with any other web3 wallet.
  • •CoinGecko, for the fiat prices shown next to your balances, and for the token list the swap picker searches. Only token identifiers are sent, never your address. Turn off Show fiat values in Settings and no price request is made; the token list is fetched only when you open the swap picker.
  • •Block explorers (Etherscan, Blockstream, mempool.space, Tronscan, Solscan, Suiscan, Stellar.Expert, ordinals.com, Mintscan, the Injective explorer, Runescan, the Aptos explorer, XRPScan, litecoinspace, Blockchair and the Canopy explorer), only when you tap a link to open one.
  • •DefiLlama, for the price chart on an asset's card when Hyperliquid's own market data does not cover that coin. Only coin identifiers are sent, never your address, and like every price request it stops when Show fiat values is off.
  • •Token logo hosts. For a coin you hold that we do not ship a logo for, the extension fetches the logo once from the https host the public token catalogue names for it (typically CoinGecko's image CDN or the token list's own host), then caches it locally forever. One request per token, ever; it carries the token's identifier, never your address.
  • •LI.FI and Jupiter, when you ask for a swap or bridge quote. They receive the pair, the amount and the address the funds move between. SunSwap quotes on Tron are read on-chain through the Tron node and send nothing to SunSwap.
  • •Hyperliquid, if you open the Perps tab. It sees your address and IP. The tab can be hidden entirely in Settings, and nothing is sent while it is.
  • •The Xverse ordinals indexer (api-3.xverse.app), to tell ordinary bitcoin from inscriptions before a Bitcoin send. It receives the Bitcoin address it is asked about and your IP address.
  • •Public token logos in lists. The swap picker and the Manage tokens list show the public logos of the tokens they list. These load as ordinary images when the list is displayed, from CoinGecko's image CDN, raw.githubusercontent.com (GitHub) and app.hyperliquid.xyz, the only image hosts the extension allows. The request carries no address and no referrer; the host sees your IP address and which public logos were shown.
  • •TronGrid, Solana, Sui and Bitcoin nodes, and Stellar's Horizon and Soroban RPC servers, for the balances, swap paths and broadcasts of those chains, on the same terms as the EVM RPC nodes. On Tron, when TronGrid is unavailable, the public full nodes of PublicNode and TronStack take over; on Bitcoin, the public Bitcore and BlockCypher indexers do.
  • •Cosmos, Aptos, XRP Ledger, Litecoin and Dogecoin nodes, for the balances and broadcasts of those chains, on the same terms as the EVM RPC nodes: the public REST gateways of the Cosmos chains (cosmos.directory, publicnode, polkachu, Ecostake, the Osmosis LCD at lcd.osmosis.zone, the Injective sentry), the Aptos Labs fullnodes and Sentio, the XRPL cluster nodes (xrplcluster.com, xrpl.ws, xrpl.link, s1/s2.ripple.com) and dRPC, litecoinspace and the public Bitcore and BlockCypher indexers for Litecoin, and the public Blockbook, Bitcore and BlockCypher indexers for Dogecoin. Each sees the public address it is asked about and your IP address.
  • •Canopy nodes (the public nodefleet nodes the Canopy explorer itself uses), only if you open the Canopy vaults screen: they see the vault addresses you query and your IP address.

These third parties may observe your public address and IP address as an inherent part of how any blockchain wallet works. Their handling of that data is governed by their privacy policies, not ours.

The one request that reaches us

The optional on-device AI assistant downloads its model from lockerprotocol.com (about 550 MB, once). It is opt-in: nothing is fetched until you accept it, and never before you have accepted the Terms of Use. The download is a plain file transfer of a public model, the same bytes for everyone, and our host records no more than any web server does for a static file. Once downloaded, the assistant runs entirely on your device: what you sign is analysed locally and never leaves the browser. You can delete the model at any time from Settings.

Apart from that download and the vault web app you choose to install, the extension makes no requests to any server operated by Locker Protocol.

5. What is sent to third parties, and your consent

A wallet cannot show a balance or send a transaction without talking to the network. To do so the extension sends financial information to the third-party services listed in section 4: your public addresses and the balances looked up for them, the transactions you sign so they can be broadcast, and the pair, amount and addresses of a swap or bridge quote you request. Each of those services also sees your IP address. None of this is sent to Locker Protocol, and none of it includes a private key or a recovery phrase, which never exist in the extension.

On Firefox this is declared to the browser as the financial and payment information data category. Firefox 140 and later ask for your consent themselves when you install the extension. On Firefox 128 to 139 the extension asks you itself, in the tab that opens after installation and again in its popup, before its first request: until you accept, no request leaves your browser. Declining is a real answer: it is kept, and the extension then sends nothing at all.

You can withdraw this consent at any time: open the extension, then Settings, Data sent to third parties, Withdraw. Every request stops at once and the question is asked again. Removing the extension also ends all transmission and erases what it stored.

6. Broad host access, explained

The extension requests access to all websites (http://*/* and https://*/*) for one reason only: to inject the standard window.ethereum provider so that any dApp you visit can request a connection, the same model every general-purpose web3 wallet (e.g. MetaMask) uses. It is not used to read page content, track browsing, or inject anything else.

7. What we do NOT do

  • ✓No analytics, telemetry, or usage tracking.
  • ✓No advertising and no third-party trackers.
  • ✓No selling, renting, or sharing of any data.
  • ✓No accounts, sign-ups, or emails required to use the extension.
  • ✓No collection of personally identifiable information.

8. Children

The extension is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect any data from them.

9. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we will update the date at the top of this page and, for material changes, note it in the extension's release notes.
